# Journal of Cleaner Production — Title and Abstract Package

## Recommended title

**Enterprise AI adoption and greenhouse-gas intensity in Europe: Sectoral evidence from fixed effects and explainable machine learning**

## Alternative titles

1. **Does enterprise AI adoption reduce greenhouse-gas intensity? Evidence from European country-sector panels**
2. **Artificial intelligence and cleaner production in Europe: Enterprise adoption, carbon intensity, and sectoral structure**
3. **AI diffusion and the green transition: European sectoral evidence on greenhouse-gas intensity and digital rebound**

## Recommended abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) may improve production and energy efficiency, yet its computing requirements and induced activity can also increase electricity demand. This study tests whether enterprise AI adoption is associated with greenhouse-gas (GHG) emission intensity across European country-sector panels. Eurostat enterprise ICT statistics on the share of firms using AI are linked to official air-emission intensity accounts using exact country, NACE Rev. 2 sector, and year identifiers for 2021, 2023, and 2024. Two-way fixed-effects models evaluate a carbon-reducing association and a pre-specified U-shaped digital rebound relationship, controlling for renewable-energy share, real GDP per capita, and R&D intensity. The econometric analysis is complemented by XGBoost with country-grouped cross-validation and grouped SHAP interpretation. AI adoption is not significantly associated with lower GHG intensity in baseline or controlled fixed-effects models, and the hypothesised U-shaped rebound effect is not supported. XGBoost nevertheless predicts out-of-country variation strongly (pooled log-outcome R² = 0.87). Grouped SHAP analysis ranks NACE sector, real GDP per capita, renewable-energy share, and R&D intensity above AI adoption in predictive importance. The evidence indicates that, during the early phase of rapid AI diffusion, sectoral production structure and energy-economic conditions dominate the measurable short-run relationship between enterprise AI adoption and GHG intensity. These findings caution against assuming that AI diffusion automatically translates into cleaner production.

## Keywords

Artificial intelligence; cleaner production; greenhouse-gas intensity; digital rebound; green transition; fixed effects; XGBoost; SHAP

## Positioning sentence for editors

The manuscript provides direct enterprise-adoption evidence rather than relying on AI patents or composite technology indices, and links this measure to official sector-level environmental accounts.
