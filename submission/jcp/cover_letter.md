# Cover Letter — Journal of Cleaner Production

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Enterprise AI adoption and greenhouse-gas intensity in Europe: Sectoral evidence from fixed effects and explainable machine learning,”** for publication in the *Journal of Cleaner Production*.

The paper examines whether the rapid diffusion of artificial intelligence among European enterprises is already associated with cleaner production outcomes. We link Eurostat enterprise ICT statistics on AI adoption to official greenhouse-gas emission-intensity accounts at the country × NACE sector × year level. The empirical design combines two-way fixed effects, nonlinear digital-rebound tests, macro-energy controls, country-grouped XGBoost validation, and grouped SHAP interpretation.

The study offers three contributions. First, it uses directly observed enterprise AI adoption rather than patent-based or composite measures of AI development. Second, it links this measure to official sector-level environmental accounts while avoiding incompatible aggregation of NACE intensity ratios. Third, it evaluates the same substantive question using both econometric and explainable machine-learning evidence.

The main finding is deliberately policy-relevant: in the currently observable 2021, 2023, and 2024 European panel, enterprise AI adoption is not robustly associated with lower greenhouse-gas intensity, and the pre-specified U-shaped digital rebound effect is not supported. At the same time, the predictive model generalises strongly across countries, while grouped SHAP values indicate that sector structure, real GDP per capita, renewable-energy share, and R&D intensity are more important than AI adoption in explaining greenhouse-gas intensity. These results caution against assuming that digitalisation automatically delivers cleaner production and highlight the importance of sectoral and energy-system context.

We believe the manuscript fits the *Journal of Cleaner Production* because it addresses cleaner production, energy and resource efficiency, sustainability assessment, and the environmental implications of technological change from a transdisciplinary perspective.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors will approve the submitted version.
