# Artificial Intelligence and the Green Transition: Does Enterprise AI Adoption Reduce Carbon Intensity or Create a Digital Rebound Effect in Europe?

## Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) can improve resource and energy efficiency, but the computing infrastructure and induced activity associated with AI may also increase electricity demand. This study examines whether enterprise AI adoption is associated with greenhouse-gas (GHG) emission intensity across European country-sector panels. Eurostat enterprise ICT data on the percentage of firms using AI are linked to Eurostat air-emission intensity accounts using exact country, NACE Rev. 2 sector, and year identifiers. The comparable core panel covers 2021, 2023, and 2024. Two-way fixed-effects models test both a linear carbon-reducing association and a pre-specified U-shaped digital rebound relationship, with renewable-energy share, real GDP per capita, and R&D intensity as controls. The econometric analysis is complemented by XGBoost with country-grouped cross-validation and grouped SHAP interpretation. Across baseline and controlled fixed-effects models, AI adoption is not significantly associated with lower GHG intensity, and the hypothesised U-shaped rebound effect is not supported. XGBoost nevertheless predicts out-of-country variation strongly (pooled log-outcome R² approximately 0.87). Grouped SHAP analysis ranks NACE sector, real GDP per capita, renewable-energy share, and R&D intensity above AI adoption in predictive importance. The findings suggest that, during the early phase of rapid AI diffusion, sectoral production structure and energy-economic conditions dominate the measurable relationship between enterprise AI adoption and carbon intensity. The results should be interpreted as an absence of robust short-run evidence for an independent AI green effect, rather than as evidence that AI has no environmental consequences.
